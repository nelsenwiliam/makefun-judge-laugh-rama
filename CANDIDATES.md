# Candidate lines — judged against the brief

Criteria, most important first: (1) office joke that lands, (2) true to
office life, (3) surprising turn, (4) tight, (5) original.

| # | Line | Verdict |
|---|------|---------|
| 1 | Our daily standup is fifteen minutes of nine people explaining why yesterday's fifteen-minute standup changed nothing. | **WINNER** — true ritual, recursive surprise, tight, fresh angle. 119 chars. |
| 2 | My boss scheduled a meeting about having too many meetings, then booked a follow-up to discuss the meeting. | Strong turn, but "meeting about meetings" is a known trope — loses on originality. |
| 3 | The printer only jams when the document is due in five minutes and the IT guy is on holiday. | True, but the setup/punch is a familiar complaint, weak surprise. |
| 4 | I put "per my last email" in a message today and felt my soul file for early retirement. | Good observation, but the punch needs a beat to parse — not instant. |
| 5 | Our quarterly goals are like the office plants: announced with great fanfare, then quietly left to die by the window. | Nice metaphor, but "office plants die" is well-worn. |
| 6 | The meeting could have been an email, but the email would have been a meeting about the meeting. | Snappy, but the trope is one of the most repeated office lines. |
| 7 | I asked IT for a second monitor and got a lecture on mindfulness instead. | Surprising, but thin — the observation is generic, not specific. |
| 8 | The Wi-Fi password changes monthly; it is the only thing in this office that ever gets updated. | True and tight, but the punch is mild — a shrug, not a laugh. |
| 9 | HR called it a fun team-building exercise; we called it trust falls with people we only know from CC. | Funny, but "trust falls" is a dated reference — freshness suffers. |
| 10 | The office coffee tastes like it was brewed by someone who lost a bet with the machine. | Decent image, but coffee jokes are the most crowded office lane. |

Final entry: candidate 1. Specific numbers (fifteen minutes, nine people)
make it concrete; the recursion (a standup about the standup) is the
surprise; it reads aloud in one breath.
